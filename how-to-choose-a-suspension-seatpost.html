How to Choose a Suspension Seatpost

A practical buyer's guide to diameters, lengths, materials and travel — so you order the right seatpost the first time, whether for one bike or a full container.

Step 1 · Measure the Seatpost Diameter

The single most important number. A suspension seatpost must match your frame's seat tube inner diameter — or the diameter of the seatpost you are replacing.

1

How to measure

  • Remove your current seatpost and measure the outer diameter with a caliper (mm).
  • No caliper? Check the number usually stamped near the bottom of the seatpost.
  • When in doubt, send us a photo of the measurement — we confirm before you order.
DiameterInchesTypical fit
25.4 mm1.0"Older road and commuter bikes, some single-speed frames
27.2 mm1.07"The most common size — classic road and mountain bikes
28.6 mm1.13"Older MTB frames and some hybrid / cruiser models
31.8 mm1.25"Modern mountain and e-bike frames
33.9 mm1.33"E-bikes, fat bikes and heavier-duty setups

Tip: if your bike is 25.4 mm it is not interchangeable with a 28.6 mm post — the fit has to be exact. Measure, don't guess.

Step 2 · Choose the Length

Seatpost length is about how deep the post inserts into the frame plus how much is exposed for your saddle height.

2

Kaber standard lengths: 300 / 350 / 400 / 450 / 500 mm

  • 300–350 mm: commuter and city bikes, average rider height.
  • 400–450 mm: mountain bikes and taller riders.
  • 500 mm: long seat tubes, cargo bikes and very tall riders.

Not sure? Measure your old post from the saddle rail clamp to the bottom, and tell us your frame size — we match it in the quote.

Step 3 · Iron or Aluminum?

Iron Alloy

  • Sturdy and durable for standard commuter and MTB use
  • More cost-effective — a good value choice
  • Available on 25.4, 27.2 and 28.6 mm models

Aluminum Alloy

  • Lighter and corrosion-resistant
  • Ideal for e-bikes, fat bikes and heavier setups
  • Available on 31.8 and 33.9 mm models

Step 4 · Straight or Flippable?

Both types are available. The choice depends on how your saddle and seat tube are set up.

Straight Type

The classic one-piece design. Simple, low-maintenance and compatible with standard seatpost clamps. A safe default for most bikes.

Flippable Type

Can be installed 180° flipped, which lets you adjust the angle between the suspension unit and the saddle — useful for unusual frame geometry or specific rider setups. Also great for buyers who want one SKU to cover more configurations.

Step 5 · Travel, Elasticity and the 100 kg Limit

Comfort comes from the suspension stroke matching your weight and terrain.

3

Set it to your rider

  • Elasticity is adjustable on the Kaber range — tune it to rider weight, cargo load and riding surface.
  • Verified max load: 100 kg (220 lb). We test and state the honest limit, because a suspension seatpost pushed past its rating fails early.
Honest limit:

Above 100 kg rider + cargo weight, a suspension seatpost is not recommended — choose a rigid post or a heavier-duty solution instead.

Common Questions

25.4 vs 28.6 — which one do I need?

Neither is "better" — they fit different frames. Measure your frame's seat tube inner diameter (or your current post's outer diameter): if it's 25.4 mm, you need a 25.4 mm post; if it's 28.6 mm, you need 28.6 mm. The two are not interchangeable.

My seatpost won't fit — did I order the wrong size?

Check diameter first, then length and saddle clamp style. If anything is off, contact us with a photo of the measurement — we confirm and replace before shipping.

Can I order a mix of sizes and types in one container?

Yes. Mixed diameters, straight + flippable, and mixed lengths ship in one order — a common way our customers test the market with a single container.

Do you make OEM versions with my logo?

Yes — logo marking, finish color and elasticity settings are customizable. Send your spec and we quote with samples.
